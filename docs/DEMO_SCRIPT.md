# The Demo

Ninety seconds. This is the product's actual sales pitch — the code exists to make this moment possible.

---

## Setup, five minutes before

- Tablet charged, `npm run seed:demo` already run, app installed to the home screen and launched standalone
- Barcode scanner plugged in and tested on two items
- Receipt printer connected, one test receipt printed and discarded
- Your phone on the owner dashboard, logged in, screen awake
- Wifi hotspot on your phone, so you control the network rather than the venue
- App already open on the sale screen. Never demo a cold start.

Hand them the tablet at the start. Someone holding a device is participating; someone watching a screen is being sold to.

---

## The script

**1. Hand it over.** *(10 sec)*

> "This is your shop. Ring something up."

Let them tap or scan. Don't explain the interface — if it needs explaining, that's a bug, and you want to see whether it does.

**2. A real sale.** *(20 sec)*

> "Add two or three more things. Now take cash — say they give you 200."

Change is calculated. Receipt prints. Let them pick it up; the physical receipt does more work than any slide.

**3. The moment.** *(15 sec)*

Reach over and switch off the hotspot mid-transaction. Say nothing at first. Let them keep going and notice on their own that nothing happened.

> "The internet just went off. Keep selling."

They will. Point at the sync pill, now ochre.

> "It's counting. Three sales saved. Nothing is lost."

**4. Offline trading.** *(15 sec)*

> "Do two more."

The pill counts up. This is the part they'll describe to another shop owner later, so let it breathe. Don't fill the silence.

**5. Reconnection.** *(15 sec)*

Switch the hotspot back on. The pill drains to green within fifteen seconds.

> "That's it. It caught up by itself. Your cashier didn't have to do anything — they wouldn't even know it happened."

**6. The owner view.** *(15 sec)*

Hand them your phone.

> "This is what you see from home. Today's sales. All five are there."

Then stop talking.

## Then stop

The demo is over. Do not walk them through reports, settings, or the product catalogue. Do not mention the feature roadmap.

Ask one question and wait:

> "ስንት ዓይነት ዕቃ አለዎት?" — "How many products do you carry?"

It's a practical question about their shop, so it's easy to answer, and the answer tells you the size of the onboarding job. More importantly, answering it moves them from watching a demo to imagining the install. Then let them ask the next question. What they ask first tells you what actually matters to them, and it's rarely what you'd have guessed.

---

## Handling the three objections you'll get

**"How much?"**
Give the number plainly, then go quiet. 500 birr a month, 2,500 to set up. Don't justify it and don't start listing features — the price is not the objection, and stacking value on top of a number you've just apologized for makes it sound expensive.

**"What if it breaks?"**
Honest answer: the data lives on their tablet, so the app breaking doesn't lose their sales. Offer a weekly export. Don't promise 24-hour support you can't staff.

**"Can it do [thing not in v1]?"**
"Not yet — that's next." Write it down in front of them. Never build it on the spot, and never say yes to something you haven't scoped. A written note reads as a roadmap; an improvised yes reads as desperation and becomes a commitment you'll resent.
